Massage therapy SOAP note template, with a real example

A SOAP note records one session in four parts. Below is a blank template you can print, a fully written example, the abbreviations most therapists use, and a way to write the whole thing in under five minutes.

What goes in each section

  • S — Subjective: what the client tells you. Their complaint in their words, where it hurts, how long, what makes it better or worse, their pain rating, and anything new in their health history.
  • O — Objective: what you observe and palpate. Posture, tissue texture, hypertonicity, trigger points, range of motion, and the techniques and areas you actually worked.
  • A — Assessment: how the tissue responded and what changed. Pain after the session, range of motion after, how the client tolerated pressure.
  • P — Plan: what happens next. When they should return, home care you gave, and any referral.

The test for a good note: could another therapist pick up treatment from it tomorrow? If yes, it's long enough.

A complete example

Sixty-minute deep tissue session for a client with neck and shoulder tension from desk work.

ClientJ.L., 34
DateOct 14
Length · Modality60 min · Deep tissue
Pain before → after6/10 → 3/10
S

C/o stiff neck on waking for 2 wks, worse after 8 h/day at a desk. Pain 6/10, R > L. Headaches 2×/wk at base of skull. No numbness or tingling. No new meds. Requested firm pressure.

O

Forward head posture, R shoulder elevated. HT B/L upper trapezius, R > L. TP R levator scapulae, referral to base of skull reproduced client's headache. Cervical rotation limited to L. Tx: deep tissue and TP release to upper trap, levator scapulae, suboccipitals; myofascial release to pectoralis minor.

A

Tissue responded well; HT reduced in upper trap. Cervical rotation to L improved. Pain 3/10 post-session. Tolerated firm pressure. Mild soreness expected 24–48 h.

P

Return in 2 wks. Home care: upper trap and pec doorway stretch 3 × 30 s daily, heat 15 min evenings, screen at eye level. Refer to PCP if headaches increase or numbness develops.

Mark the body chart, not just the text

A body chart turns three sentences into one glance. Mark pain, tension and areas to avoid on the anterior and posterior views, using the client's left and right. On the anterior view, the client's left is on your right; that flip is the most common charting mistake.

How to write it in five minutes

  1. Write S during intake, while the client is talking. Their words are the note.
  2. Right after the session, tap the body chart first: it jogs your memory for O.
  3. Use the same short phrases every time (“HT B/L upper trap”, “tolerated firm pressure”). Consistency makes notes faster to write and easier to compare.
  4. Write P before the client leaves, so the home care you mention is the home care you record.

FAQ

Do massage therapists have to write SOAP notes?

It depends on your state board, your liability insurer, and whether you bill insurance. Many therapists chart every session regardless, because notes are their best protection if a complaint or claim comes up. Check your state's rules.

How long should a massage SOAP note be?

Long enough that another therapist could continue treatment from it. For most sessions that's four to eight short lines plus a body chart.

How long should I keep client records?

Retention periods vary by state and by insurer. Find your state's requirement and keep an exported copy for at least that long.
