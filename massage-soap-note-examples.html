Massage SOAP note examples: five real sessions, fully charted

A template tells you what the boxes are; examples show what to write in them. Here are five common sessions charted the way a busy therapist would chart them between clients, with a note on why each line is there.

  1. Upper back and shoulders, desk worker
  2. Low back tightness after yard work
  3. Post-race leg recovery
  4. Prenatal, side-lying
  5. Client-reported tension headaches

Two rules run through every example: write what the client reports and what you observe, and never write a diagnosis of your own. If a client has a diagnosis, record it as theirs: “client reports dx of … from PCP.”

1. Upper back and shoulders, desk worker

Returning client, third visit. The point of this note is comparison: it refers back to last visit so progress is visible.

ClientR.M., 41
Visit3rd
Length · Modality60 min · Swedish + TP
Pain before → after5/10 → 2/10
S

Upper back “burning” between shoulder blades by end of workday, 5/10 (was 7/10 at visit 1). Started using a laptop stand after last visit. No new meds or injuries. Prefers medium pressure.

O

Rounded shoulders, less pronounced than visit 1. HT B/L rhomboids and mid trap, R > L. TP R rhomboid major, local tenderness only, no referral. Tx: Swedish full back, TP release R rhomboid, pin and stretch upper trap, pec minor MFR.

A

HT decreased in rhomboids; TP softened after 3 rounds. Pain 2/10 post. Client says this is the best the area has felt since starting.

P

Continue every 2 wks for 2 more visits, then reassess spacing. Home care: doorway pec stretch 2× daily, shoulder blade squeezes on breaks.

Body chart: posterior view, tension mark B/L between scapulae, trigger point R rhomboid.

Why it works: the pain number and posture are compared to visit 1, and the home care from last time (laptop stand) is followed up. That's the thread that shows treatment is going somewhere.

2. Low back tightness after yard work

New client with a recent, specific cause. The screening questions matter most here, because they show you checked before working.

ClientD.K., 56
Visit1st
Length · Modality60 min · Deep tissue
Pain before → after6/10 → 3/10
S

Low back tight and achy since raking leaves 4 days ago, 6/10, worse bending forward and getting out of the car. Denies pain or tingling down the legs. Denies fever or numbness. Has not seen a provider for it. Taking OTC ibuprofen.

O

Guarded movement getting onto table. HT B/L lumbar paraspinals and QL, L > R. TTP L QL and L glute med. Tx: prone, moderate-to-deep pressure lumbar paraspinals, QL, glutes; side-lying QL stretch on L. Avoided deep work directly on spine.

A

Paraspinals softened; client moved off table more easily. Pain 3/10 post. Pressure kept moderate on L QL per client feedback.

P

Return in 1 wk if still tight. Home care: gentle knee-to-chest and walking. Advised to see PCP if pain spreads down a leg, numbness appears, or it isn't improving within a couple of weeks.

Body chart: posterior view, pain mark L low back, tension mark B/L lumbar, tender spot L hip.

Why it works: “denies pain down the legs” and the referral line record that you screened and set limits. Note the pain reliever too, since it can change how pressure feels to the client.

3. Post-race leg recovery

A sports session two days after a half marathon. Notes like this are short, but timing and pressure are worth recording.

ClientA.T., 29
VisitRegular
Length · Modality60 min · Sports
Soreness before → after5/10 → 3/10
S

Ran a half marathon 2 days ago. Calves and quads sore, 5/10, stairs hardest. No sharp pain, no swelling noticed. Next run planned in 4 days.

O

HT B/L gastrocnemius and soleus, B/L quads, R IT band area TTP. No visible swelling or bruising. Tx: light-to-moderate flushing strokes, compression, broad cross-fiber on calves and quads, gentle ROM at hip and ankle. Kept pressure light given timing after race.

A

Legs felt “lighter” per client. Soreness 3/10 post. Ankle dorsiflexion felt freer on passive ROM.

P

Easy walk today, hydrate. Return before next race for a deeper session. Client to mention any sharp or one-spot pain next visit.

Body chart: anterior view, tension B/L quads; posterior view, tension B/L calves; tender spot R lateral thigh.

Why it works: it says why pressure was light. If the client later asks why this session felt different from the last one, the reason is in the record.

4. Prenatal, side-lying

Prenatal notes put positioning and the client's own provider front and center. Follow your training and your insurer's guidance on prenatal work; the note below just shows what to record.

ClientS.P., 33
Visit2nd
Length · Modality60 min · Prenatal
Discomfort before → after4/10 → 2/10
S

Client reports 26 wks pregnant. Says her OB knows she's getting massage and raised no concerns. Low back and hip ache by evening, 4/10. Reports no new symptoms or changes since last visit. Sleeping on L side.

O

Positioned side-lying L then R with pillows between knees, under head and abdomen. HT B/L glute med and lumbar paraspinals. Tx: Swedish, light-to-moderate pressure to back, hips, shoulders and legs. Legs: light strokes only, no deep work.

A

Comfortable in side-lying throughout; repositioned once at client's request. Discomfort 2/10 post. Got up slowly without dizziness.

P

Return in 3 wks. Ask about any changes from her OB at each visit. Client to tell OB about the hip ache at her next appointment.

Body chart: posterior view, tension B/L hips and low back; note “side-lying only” in precautions.

Why it works: the client's own report about her provider is recorded in her words, positioning is spelled out, and the plan sends her questions back to her provider rather than answering them yourself.

5. Client-reported tension headaches

Headaches are a common reason clients book. Chart the pattern the client describes, not a label.

ClientL.W., 47
Visit1st
Length · Modality45 min · Neck focus
Pain before → after3/10 → 1/10
S

Headaches “like a band” across forehead and temples 3–4×/wk, late afternoons, for about 2 months. Client reports PCP told her they're tension headaches. Mild one today, 3/10. Jaw clenching noticed by dentist.

O

Forward head posture. HT B/L suboccipitals, upper trap, SCM; TTP B/L temporalis and masseter. Tx: supine, suboccipital release, gentle SCM work, upper trap kneading, temporalis and masseter (external only), scalp massage.

A

Client reports headache 1/10 by end. Jaw felt “looser.” Tolerated light-to-moderate pressure on face; firm on upper trap.

P

Return in 1 wk, then reassess. Client to keep a note of headache days to compare next visit. Contact PCP if headaches change in pattern or get worse.

Body chart: anterior view, pain mark forehead/temples, tension jaw B/L; posterior view, tension suboccipitals and upper trap.

Why it works: “client reports PCP told her” keeps the diagnosis where it belongs. Asking the client to track headache days gives you a real number to compare next time.

What these examples have in common

  • A before and after number, even for relaxation clients. It's the simplest way to show change over a series of visits.
  • The client's words in quotes where they're vivid (“burning”, “like a band”). They're more useful than your paraphrase.
  • Techniques and areas, not just a modality. “Deep tissue” alone doesn't tell the next reader what you did.
  • Why you held back. Light pressure, avoided areas and positioning choices are the lines that matter if a session is ever questioned.
  • A plan the client heard. Home care and referral advice recorded as given, before they leave.

What you're required to record, and how long to keep it, depends on your state board, your liability insurer and whether you bill insurance. Check your board's rules and your policy documents; these examples aren't a substitute for either.
